# The FedRAMP Rev5 Agency Authorization Path - Summary This documentation outlines the FedRAMP agency authorization process from a Cloud Service Provider's perspective, organized into three phases. ## Phase 1: Preparation **FedRAMP Ready:** An optional but recommended designation obtained through a Readiness Assessment with a FedRAMP-recognized Third Party Assessor (3PAO). The resulting report documents the CSP's capability to meet federal security requirements and enables listing on the FedRAMP Marketplace. **Pre-Authorization encompasses three elements:** 1. **Partnership Establishment:** CSPs formalize relationships with federal agencies by completing a CSP Information Form, which generates a FedRAMP ID and provides resources. The documentation addresses common questions about initial agency partnerships, continuous monitoring responsibilities, and service discontinuation scenarios. 2. **Authorization Planning:** CSPs must confirm dedicated resources, select a 3PAO, and choose between two review approaches: "Just-In-Time Linear" (iterative, recommended) or simultaneous submission of all deliverables. Organizations submit In-Process Requests (IPR) and Work Breakdown Structures (WBS) to FedRAMP for marketplace listing eligibility. 3. **Kickoff Meeting:** Introduces key stakeholders, reviews the cloud service offering's purpose, authorization boundary, security gaps, and remediation plans while establishing shared understanding of timelines and responsibilities. ## Phase 2: Authorization **Full Security Assessment:** The 3PAO develops a Security Assessment Plan (SAP), conducts testing while the system remains frozen from development changes, and produces a Security Assessment Report (SAR) with findings and authorization recommendations. CSPs then develop Plans of Action & Milestones (POA&M) addressing identified risks, followed by a SAR debrief presentation. **Agency Authorization Process:** The federal agency reviews deliverables and either approves them or requests additional testing. Upon approval, the agency's Authorizing Official issues an ATO letter. FedRAMP then performs quality and risk reviews to determine suitability for government-wide reuse, ultimately granting a "FedRAMP Authorized" designation. This designation enables other federal agencies to request security packages and issue their own ATOs based on FedRAMP's assessment. ## Phase 3: Continuous Monitoring CSPs must demonstrate mature continuous monitoring capabilities during assessment and authorization reviews. Post-authorization, CSPs submit monthly ConMon deliverables including updated POA&Ms, vulnerability scans, deviation requests, and incident reports. Organizations with multiple federal agency customers implement collaborative ConMon approaches to streamline oversight. Annual security assessments by independent 3PAOs are mandatory to maintain acceptable risk posture throughout the system's lifecycle.
